# Groundwork 0.14.0: the 3D view

## Your profiles in QGIS's 3D view

**Groundwork → Show in 3D…** stands what you tick in QGIS's own 3D map view: the models as
curtains under their profiles, at their real elevation, with the boreholes, stations and
your interpretation around them. Nothing is shown until you ask, and nothing of it is stored
in your Groundwork file. See [The 3D view](../user/howto/3d-view.md) for all of it.

- **Models as curtains**, each in the look you choose under **Show As**:
    - **As in the Section**: the section's display, so cells stay cells, a smooth model
      becomes a point cloud and contours become 3D lines, in the section's colour scale.
      You can style them in QGIS and **Identify** their values.
    - **Point Cloud**: the smooth field as a point cloud, whatever the section shows.
    - **Section Picture**: the section exactly as the panel draws it, contour labels
      included, and optionally with the **interpretation painted in**. All pictured models
      of a profile are one picture.
- **Low coverage:** with the section's coverage fade on, faded cells stand see-through
  (fade mode **Fade**) or are left out (**Blank**).
- **Boreholes** stand as round columns at their map position, each interval in its colour
  from the section, with the borehole's **name** above the column.
- **Stations** stand as small spheres at their surveyed height, each profile's **ground
  line** runs along the top of its curtain, and its **name** floats above its start.
- **The interpretation** stands at the depth you drew it, in its 2D colours, transparency
  and outlines, and follows when you restyle it in 2D. An edit in the section shows in 3D as
  soon as it is saved.
- **A ready 3D view:** if none is open, one opens with the terrain off, eye-dome lighting on
  and the camera on what you ticked. A 3D view you already have open is left as it is.
- **Apply** shows your choice and keeps the dialog open, so you can compare looks.

## 3D and section, linked

- **Point at a curtain**, a picture, a point cloud or a borehole column in the 3D view: the
  section panel's cursor and the map's marker move to that place, with the elevation and the
  value in the readout.
- **Click** on another profile's curtain and the section panel opens that profile.
- **Right-click the profile list** in the section panel: **Look at Profile in 3D** turns the
  3D view side-on to the profile; **Cut 3D View Along Profile** cuts the view down to a slab
  along it, so the curtain shows through a terrain or DEM you keep switched on. **Remove 3D
  Cut** shows everything again.

## Good to know

- The 3D layers sit in one collapsed **3D View** group in the Layers panel.
- Point clouds and section pictures are files in a folder next to your Groundwork file,
  named after it with `_3d` at the end. They are made from your models: if the folder is
  missing (not copied along), QGIS asks about the unavailable layers; keep them, and they
  are written again.
- The 3D layers are kept up to date while a 3D view is open. If you work without one, they
  catch up as soon as a 3D view opens, or when you press **Apply** or **OK** in **Show in
  3D…**.
- **Qgis2threejs** reads the same layers; the guide says how.

## Also changed

- Reinstalling the plugin while the section panel is open no longer shows an error.

## Updating

Install `groundwork_geophysics-0.14.0.zip` with **Plugins → Manage and Install Plugins →
Install from ZIP**, over 0.13, then restart QGIS. Your Groundwork files are not changed by
this update. When you open a project, its stations and interpretation layers get a 3D style
(once; a 3D style of your own stays): save the project to keep it.

## What to try

1. Unzip the Erfurt demo and open `erfurt_project.qgz`.
2. Choose **Groundwork → Show in 3D…**, press **Select All** and **Apply**. A 3D view opens
   on the seven curtains.
3. Set one model's **Show As** to **Section Picture**, tick **Section pictures show the
   interpretation** and press **Apply** again: that curtain now looks exactly like its
   section.
4. Open a profile in the section panel and move the mouse over its curtain in the 3D view:
   the section's cursor follows. Click another curtain: the panel opens that profile.
5. Right-click the profile list → **Cut 3D View Along Profile**, then **Remove 3D Cut**.
6. Draw a polygon on the section: it stands in the 3D view as soon as you finish it.
7. In the Gülpe demo, tick a profile's **Boreholes**: the cores stand as columns with their
   names.
