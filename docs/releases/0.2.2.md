# Geophysics Workbench 0.2.2: a real-data demo (Gülpe, Havel)

Until now the demo was synthetic. 0.2.2 adds a second demo built entirely from real, openly
published data from the Lower Havel floodplain at Gülpe, Brandenburg:

- four ERT transects with the authors' own BERT inversions;
- 24 sediment cores;
- the state geological survey's boreholes and geological map, loaded live from its web
  services.

It shows what having everything in QGIS gives you: the section next to the map, real cores on
the real resistivity section, and third-party data in the same view, without exporting
anything by hand.

## What's new

- **The Gülpe demo** (`geophysics_workbench-demo-guelpe-0.2.2.zip`):
  - Transects 1–4 (207–259 m, 0.5–1 m electrode spacing) with the published BERT models, unchanged.
  - The electrodes at their surveyed GPS positions.
  - The cores as boreholes, labelled by grain-size class and texture code, for example
    "sand · St2". 21 of them lie on the transects.
  - Live from Brandenburg:
    - the LBGR borehole map, with and without layer logs;
    - the GÜK300 geology as the band above the section;
    - aerial images.
  - The 1 m elevation model (DGM1) for topography.
  - Rivers and ditches from OpenStreetMap as crossing lines.
- **Point layers can have a depth.** In **Context layers…**, each point layer has a new
  depth field. Points with a depth are drawn on the section as a thin stick down to that
  depth. The Gülpe demo uses this for the state boreholes (`Teufe_in_m`).
- **A layer log is one click away.** In the Gülpe project, a state borehole's scanned layer
  log opens from the map via the layer action **Open layer log** (right-click the feature, or
  use the attribute table).

## How to update

1. Install `geophysics_workbench-0.2.2.zip` with **Plugins → Manage and Install Plugins… →
   Install from ZIP** (German QGIS: **Erweiterungen → … → Aus ZIP installieren**), then restart
   QGIS. It replaces 0.2.1.
2. Unzip `geophysics_workbench-demo-guelpe-0.2.2.zip` into a new, empty folder and open
   `guelpe_project.qgz`. The synthetic demo is unchanged.

**The state layers need an internet connection.** Without one, the sections, cores, elevation
model and waterways still work; only the live layers stay empty.

## What to try

1. **Open Transect 3 and look at the cores.** Pick Transect 3 (**Geophysics Workbench → Pick
   profile on map**). Nine cores (RK10–RK18) stand in the section along the line. Compare the
   clay and peat layers with the low resistivities just below the surface.
2. **Move along the section and watch the map.** The cursor position on the section is shown
   on the map, next to the aerial image, the ditches and the state boreholes.
3. **Open a state borehole's layer log.** On the map, right-click a red diamond (**State
   boreholes with log**) and choose **Open layer log**. The scanned log opens in the browser.
4. **Give your own point layer a depth.** In **Context layers…**, pick a numeric field as the
   depth of a point layer and click **Apply**. The points become sticks on the section.

## Known limits

- Sand cores are drawn in yellow, which is close to the yellow of the default resistivity
  colour ramp. Colours become adjustable with the styling panel planned for a later release.
- The three cores taken in 2024 (RK22–RK24) are 260–300 m from any transect. They show on the
  map only.
- If Brandenburg's services are slow or down, their layers stay empty. The demo itself still
  works.
