# Geophysics Workbench 0.5.0: borehole viewer

Boreholes now get their own window, and they look the same on the section and in the viewer.
Their look comes from QGIS's own style editor.

## What's new

- **Borehole viewer.** A separate window that you can move anywhere, for example onto a second screen.
  - **Opening it:**
    - click a borehole column on the section (Pan mode), or a borehole of the open profile on the map;
    - when the click also hits an interpretation piece, the details show as before, with an **Open in borehole viewer** link.
  - **Content:** all boreholes of the current profile, from every borehole entry. The one you clicked is highlighted. The window follows the section when you switch profiles.
  - **Spacing:** *True distance* places each borehole at its chainage, with the ground line and **VE** shown and settable. *Even* is a correlation panel in profile order.
  - **Axis:** elevation, or depth below the top.
    - A borehole without any height is listed as "No height" and shown in depth mode only.
  - **Zoom and pan:**
    - the mouse wheel zooms around the cursor;
    - Ctrl + wheel zooms the vertical axis only, Shift + wheel the horizontal axis only;
    - drag to pan; **Fit** shows everything.
  - **Display panel:**
    - lithology, layer text, depth ticks and height flags;
    - each log curve;
    - labels (name, elevation, distance/offset) with a QGIS font button.
  - **Readout:** the borehole, depth, elevation, the interval at the cursor and the curve values, marked "(interpolated)" between samples.
  - Nothing links boreholes on its own: no correlation lines, no fills between columns.
- **Borehole style… (QGIS's own style editor).**
  - QGIS can't keep a style on a table without geometry, so the window opens QGIS's normal Categorized, Rule-based, Graduated or Single symbol editor on a temporary copy of the interval table. The copy is never saved or added to your project.
  - Classify, symbols, SVG fills, the style library and expressions all work as usual.
  - **Patterns…**: a colour and an FGDC pattern for every class in one table, with pattern previews and search. The result is ordinary QGIS symbols in the editor.
  - **Export… / Import…** a `.qml` style file to reuse a scheme in other projects.
  - **Two ready-made styles to import:** German short codes (`din_codes.qml`) and EN ISO 14688-1 symbols (`iso_14688.qml`), with FGDC patterns. Import… opens in their folder. The colours and patterns are the plugin's choice, not DIN 4023.
  - The style is saved in the project for each interval table. The section and the viewer both draw with it.
  - Unstyled intervals get a neutral colour per text. Nothing is guessed from the words: the old built-in sand/clay/peat colour table is no longer used for boreholes.
- **FGDC lithology patterns.**
  - The 116 sedimentary, metamorphic and igneous patterns of FGDC-STD-013-2006 (public domain; SVGs from davenquinn/geologic-patterns, CC0) are in every SVG fill picker while the plugin is loaded.
  - The files are named by code and name ("607 Massive sand or sandstone.svg"), so QGIS's SVG picker shows what they are.
  - They are offered, never assigned.
  - Your QGIS SVG path settings are not changed.
- **Boreholes on the section** are now the same styled columns, over the model.
  - **The viewer is the one place for how they look:**
    - its **Layer text** switch and the curves ticked under **Curves** are drawn on the section too;
    - so are the **column width** (px) and **opacity** under "On the section".
- **Several borehole entries** in Context layers…, each with:
  - its interval table;
  - an optional **total-depth** field: a borehole without intervals is drawn as an empty column to that depth;
  - any number of **curve tables**: rows of depth plus values, linked by borehole ID, e.g. a client's Excel opened in QGIS, with a unit per curve.
- **Heights.**
  - The top comes from the elevation field, else the DEM, else the drawn surface, as before. The readout now says which.
  - **New height flag:** when the elevation field and the DEM differ by more than the tolerance, a red triangle marks the top. Hovering shows both numbers.
- **Curve style…** in the viewer: colour, width, dash, fill to the left or right, log scale, and min/max (empty = data range).

## Demo (Gülpe)

- `scripts/build_demo_guelpe.py` now adds:
  - **"Synthetic curves (NOT measured)"**: seeded random gamma and conductivity values for each core, made only to show curve tracks. The cores have no real geophysical logs. The table name, its description, the field names and the units all say *synthetic*.
  - **the LBGR boreholes as a second borehole entry**, drawn to their total depth (`Teufe_in_m`) instead of as point sticks.
- **The demo zip is a fresh build** (made into a separate folder with
  `build_demo_guelpe.py --out .cache/guelpe_build`, zipped with
  `package.py --guelpe .cache/guelpe_build`). It has the synthetic curves and the new
  settings. Your own test drawings in the repository's `demo_guelpe/` are untouched and not
  shipped.

## What to try

1. Gülpe, Transect 3: click the RK16 column on the section. The viewer opens with RK16 highlighted.
2. Switch **Spacing** between *True distance* and *Even*, and the **Axis** to *Depth*. Zoom with the wheel, and with Ctrl/Shift + wheel.
3. In the viewer, open **Borehole style…**:
   1. choose *Categorized* on `lithology`, then **Classify**;
   2. give "sand" an SVG fill with FGDC pattern 607 over a yellow simple fill;
   3. press **OK**.

   The section's columns follow.
4. In the viewer, switch on **Layer text**, tick a curve, and set **On the section** to 24 px and opacity 0.7. The section follows.
5. With the synthetic curves added: tick *gamma_synthetic* in the viewer's **Curves**, then try **Curve style…** (fill left, colour).
6. Switch the section to Transect 4: the viewer follows.

## Known limits

- The FGDC patterns are drawn in black; their line colour can't be changed. Put a coloured simple fill under the SVG fill for colour.
- Even spacing measures gaps in pixels, so it shows no VE.
- `.las`, SEP3, GeODin and AGS files and an Excel import dialog are not part of this release (parked). Curves come from tables opened in QGIS.
